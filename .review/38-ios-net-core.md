## Summary
The unit is the iOS app's link to a hub: one `@MainActor` `DataClient` that owns the data/1 WebSocket, the lists and entries it folds, the disk cache, the outbox, push registration, the hub list with its home-hub failover, and small page models (devices, machines, notifications, ring, terminals, dictation upload, Google's loopback sign-in). The careful parts are the pure ones with tests shared with Android (`HubList`, `HubRescue`, `Outbox`, `TermStream`, `Migration`, `WireJSON`), the Keychain store (after first unlock, this device only), the pairing exchange (ephemeral session, no redirects, no cache) and the confirmation before a link from outside pairs anything. `DataClient` itself has no test and no seam for one, and it is where the defects are: the thread subscription is one integer shared by overlapping screens and by the reconnect sync, so a thread can be subscribed twice (doubled streamed text) or not at all; every 401 or 403 is "revoked" and the re-pair screen stays after the hub recovers; and the code added for several hubs is uneven (a tapped alert from the home hub strands the phone there, each connect wipes the other hubs' alerts, pairing a second hub sends the first one's unsent messages to it). Security: the credential is stored well but travels in the address of the socket and of file downloads through the shared cached session, and a hub's unsigned offer makes the phone send that credential to an address of the hub's choosing with no dialog. The transport exception is app-wide (`NSAllowsArbitraryLoads`) with no warning for plain http outside a tailnet. `build.sh` has no secrets in it, but two of its three modes do not do what the documents say, and `signing.env` is not ignored. The privacy manifest's three required-reason entries match what the code uses (UserDefaults, uptime, file times in the app's container). Several hundred lines of the old server-drawn app remain as dead code beside the live client.

## Cross-unit questions
- Answer (a), token in the address: yes. `/files/get` and `/bots/<id>/screen.jpg` are fetched with `?token=` through `URLSession.shared` (DataViewer.swift:76, Hub.swift:35), and the socket address carries it. `/files/get` answers `Cache-Control: no-cache`, not `no-store` (wire/mod.rs:63), so the shared on-disk cache may keep the body under an address that holds the credential; `screen.jpg` is `no-store`. The Hub screen, pairing, the device page and the recorder use a header.
- Answer (b), 401: yes. Every 401 or 403 at the socket is "revoked, pair again", and the screen stays after a later successful connect (DataClient.swift:292, 305). A 503 would be retried.
- Answer (c), the "none" frame: confirmed from this side (DataClient.swift:286, Plot.swift:466); see Feature gaps.
- Answer (d), a push after unpairing: iOS shows it without the app. A tap opens the thread id on whatever hub is in use, or the pairing screen (DataClient.swift:1155). Silent clears are applied whatever hub sent them. The app never sends `device.drop` and cannot once refused, so only the hub can stop it.
- Answer (e), 16 MiB: the limit is DataClient.swift:250; a larger frame ends the socket and the open screen asks again after the reconnect, in a loop. `attachment.get` and `message.image` (8 MiB, about 11 MiB as base64) fit.
- Answer (f), Checks: the Review tab's Checks page is on both apps. What iOS lacks is the Checks panel of the management panels beside the drawing (feature `mgmt`), with BOM, Lifecycle and Fab; see Feature gaps.
- Unit 11 and 07: which hub answers `push.state` for an alert the home hub sent while the phone is connected to the desktop's hub (DataClient.swift:1104)? Is the phone's push token carried to the home hub by the move, or only by `device.register` there?
- Unit 11: should `subscribe {topic:"thread"}` be idempotent for one socket and thread (data/mod.rs:727), given one frame is sent per subscription (line 695)?
- Unit 07 and 16: does a `setting.set` of `home.at` reach `publish_offer` (homemove.rs:682) at once or at the next start, and can the offer be limited to an address the hub has verified as the owner's (DataClient.swift:326)?
- Unit 04: may a device revoke itself on `/auth/revoke` with its own credential, so Forget can do it (Hub.swift:380 discards the `id`)? Will a storage failure answer 503 instead of 401?
- Unit 44, 45 and 11: is every frame to a data socket held under the phones' 16 MiB (plots, board models, `viewer.diff`, `thread.entries` with long texts), and is `OUTBOX_MAX` (hub.rs:53) enforced there?
- Unit 37 (Android): `closeThread` and `subscribe` share one `threadSub` in the same way (DataClient.kt:487-493), and `adopt` is at DataClient.kt:124: do the double subscription and the outbox of the hub before happen there too?
- Unit 60 (iOS views): DataMarkdown.swift:212 fetches any http(s) image address in a message with `URLSession.shared` (a tracking or exfiltration path through agent output); DataViews.swift:101 lets any outside `…://account/<id>` link open that account's sign-in with `start: true` and no question; `AskPairView` (DataViews.swift:1514) names only the link's origin.
- Docs: docs/ios-build.md ("passes it for all modes", a `backplane://pair` link from the Camera) and mobile/README.md against build.sh:42 and the registered scheme; docs/i18n/README.md:116 against the missing generator.

## Summary
`kedit.rs` turns one `kicad_edit` tool call into a sequence of KiCad IPC commands (open, read, one commit, refill, save, check before and after) and answers with short rows. `kroute.rs` is a pure grid router (A* over a bitmap of the board, vias by the board's own kinds) and a placement search. `edchecks.rs` is a different thing from what the unit prompt says: it runs DRC and ERC inside the open editor and turns markers into kicad-cli's report; the "moved finding is not new" rule lives in `kedit::check_delta`. The pure rules are well tested and the code is careful about many KiCad quirks, but the driver's hardest part (the `route` arm with rip-up, stub trimming and track breaking, `place`, the footprint exchange and the `fit` search, about 250 dense lines) has no test in the crate and holds two defects I could trace. The main risks are: unbounded work from agent-given numbers (`within`, `layers`) on the hub's own runtime and memory; atomicity that is weaker than the doc says (commits are split far more often than "a find", and three failure paths misreport what stayed in the document); `route` side effects that are larger than the request (a whole net taken up for one near pad) and results that read as success (`already_connected` by zone outline, rows echoed from the request). The lock is taken by the first write command, not for the edit, and the document edited is not compared with the path named. `edchecks.rs` is in better shape; its one real weakness is a sheet-path cache shared across projects.

## Cross-unit questions
- agents/mcp.rs:226 (unit 14): what happens to a `tool_kicad_edit` in flight when the turn is stopped or the agent process goes away?

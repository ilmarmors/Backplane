## Summary
The unit holds the hub-side decisions behind the sidebar: the status word (`signal.rs`), the shelf a thread sits on (`shelf.rs`), the project and thread lists with trees, pins, manual keys, search and paging (`sidebar.rs`), other machines' mirrored states and origins (`mirror.rs`), and the routing-plan tools and panel (`plan/`). The pure parts are careful: the signal propagation is monotone with fuel that provably suffices, parent-link cycles are cut by `chain_ok` (every member of a cycle and anything hanging off one becomes a root), and the bounded-paging plan has a strong parity test against the full projection at 12k threads. The main risks are elsewhere: the plan approval loop keeps its open requests only in process memory, keyed by a raw path string, and an ask about something the plan no longer holds is never answered, so an agent told to "end your turn and wait" can wait forever. The phones' consumer of `signal::index` and `pin_place` recomputes both for every row of a list, which scales as rows times all threads under the policy-state lock. The phones order and nest threads client-side with rules that differ from the desktop's in several ways, against the "UI is dumb" rule. Smaller issues: one event writer stores `at` in milliseconds, the urgency ranking contradicts its own comment for stalls, mirrored machine keys can collide through `short_name`, and the three docs describe tools and a web client that are not in this tree.
